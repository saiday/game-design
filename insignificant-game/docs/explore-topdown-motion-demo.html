Making units move without animating them

Every fighter below is one static sprite — the actual exploratory renders, no frame sequences. All the life comes from cheap transforms: flip for facing, position tweens for walking, a little procedural bob, and lunge / projectile attacks. Toggle each technique to see exactly what it buys.

Static sprite

Each unit is a single PNG drawn to the canvas every frame. No walk-cycle frames exist — the thing our AI pipeline can't produce consistently anyway.

cost: 1 image / unit

Facing = horizontal flip

A unit faces its target by mirroring on the X axis. Turn it off and the right-hand army marches in backwards — that's the whole job the flip does.

cost: 0 extra art (L/R)

Walk = position tween

Movement is the node sliding toward the enemy line. The sprite never changes; only its coordinates do. This is how RimWorld actually walks.

cost: a lerp

Procedural bob

A sine-wave vertical bounce plus a hair of rotation while moving. Free liveliness from math — no frames. Off = a flat glide.

cost: one sin()

Attack = lunge tween

Melee units ease forward and recoil on the hit. A transform, not an animation. Off = damage still lands, but the strike reads as dead-still.

cost: ease in/out

Projectiles

Ranged units spawn a tracer that tweens to the target and flashes on impact. The shooter itself barely moves. Off = instant silent hits.

cost: a moving dot

Why this tier. Nothing here needs a rigged skeleton or a single generated animation frame — it fits the AI sprite pipeline and mirrors how RimWorld genuinely moves. Skeletal rigging (real limb motion from paper-doll layers) is the next tier up, added only when a specific unit demands it. Frame-by-frame walk cycles stay out of scope. The one thing flip can't do is up/down facing — that needs discrete N/S sprites, and that's the real cost wall, only paid if the field needs vertical movement.
